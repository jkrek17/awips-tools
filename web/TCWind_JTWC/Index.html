Anchoring

Anchored — a reported radius constrains the fit at this range on this bearing. Extrapolation — hatched on the map: inside the innermost reported ring (JTWC never reports an RMW), and outside the outermost, where only the fitted profile and its taper decide the answer.

QuadR64 R50R34 Anchored

Radii in nm as reported. Anchored is the span between the innermost and outermost fit target in that quadrant — 0.85 × the reported radius, because the bulletin states the quadrant maximum and GTCM fits the quadrant average.

Radial profile

  • ─Line — the GTCM profile actually built, on that quadrant's bearing.
  • ◆Filled diamond — the fit target: 0.85 × reported radius, at its threshold. This is what the least-squares actually sees.
  • ◯Open circle — the radius the bulletin reports, unscaled.
  • │Stem — the wind residual at the target: modelled speed minus threshold. These are the numbers the RMS above is the root-mean-square of.

GTCM fits one symmetric vortex plus a single wavenumber-1 asymmetry to every target at once. It is not built to pass through them and it does not: the stems are the price paid for a field with no kinks at the quadrant boundaries.

Model, departures and provenance

The field is NHC's Gridded TCM (GTCM/WTCM) as described in the Gridded TCM Users Guide v1.9.1 (Santos & DeMaria, 4 Dec 2023): one symmetric modified-Rankine vortex (rm, ri, x1, x2) plus one wavenumber-1 asymmetry from the storm motion, fitted by weighted least squares on wind error — weight 5 on the 34 kt targets, 1 on 50 and 64.

Two documented departures from the guide, both deliberate:

  • Outer taper. The guide's grids simply stop outside the 34 kt radii and leave the blend to the receiving office. This tool inserts into a background field, so beyond the modelled 34 kt radius the profile is tapered exponentially. A bare Rankine tail never terminates on its own.
  • No boundary-layer or land-roughness reduction. Step 4 of the guide needs the USGS land-surface database, which this procedure does not have. The field is marine-exposure everywhere and will be too strong over land.

Previously this tool fitted each quadrant's radial profile independently and interpolated between quadrant bisectors. That hit every reported ring exactly and produced kinks at the quadrant boundaries. The office chose coherence over ring fidelity; the tool's job is a physically plausible field consistent with the bulletin, not a reproduction of the radii.

Live data: JTWC warnings from NOAA's Telecommunications Gateway (tgftp.nws.noaa.gov), cached briefly server-side. The Archive and Findings pages instead use post-season best-track reanalysis via NCEI's IBTrACS, which shows consistency with best-track analyses — a weaker claim than verification against observations, which has not been done.
