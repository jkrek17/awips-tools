What this page is. Every storm here is post-season best-track reanalysis from IBTrACS — not a live bulletin. The positions, intensities and wind radii were assigned after the fact, with data the forecaster on shift did not have. When the modelled field agrees with the plotted radii, that means consistency with an after-the-fact analysis. It is not verification against observations, and nothing on this page should be read as one. The radii themselves are analyst estimates with their own substantial uncertainty.
What "agreement" is not. GTCM deliberately does not pass through the reported radii. It fits one symmetric vortex plus a wavenumber-1 asymmetry by weighted least squares on wind error, trading ring fidelity for a coherent field. A ring miss here is the model working as designed, not a defect. Read the misses for size and structure, not as a score.

    How to read this page

    • Post-season, not live. IBTrACS best track is a reanalysis. Agreement is consistency with an after-the-fact analysis, not verification against observations.
    • GTCM is not trying to hit the rings. It minimises weighted wind error (weight 5 on the 34 kt points, 1 on 50 and 64 kt) against the reported radii scaled by 0.85 from quadrant maximum to quadrant average. Ring misses are expected and were accepted in exchange for a field without kinks at the quadrant boundaries.
    • rm is not RMW. The fitted rm is the radius of maximum symmetric tangential wind in the Rankine profile, chosen by least squares on wind error at the reported radii — frequently with no 64 kt ring and sometimes with no radii at all. The best-track RMW is an analyst's estimate of the radius of maximum surface wind, from aircraft, scatterometer, radar or satellite. They are related quantities, differently defined and differently measured. A difference between them is not by itself an error in either, and this page never scores one against the other.
    • Two documented departures from the Gridded TCM Users Guide v1.9.1, both present in the field drawn here: (1) the profile is tapered exponentially beyond the modelled 34 kt radius so the GFE insert terminates — the guide leaves the outer blend to the receiving office; (2) the guide's step 4 boundary-layer and land-roughness reduction is not implemented, as it needs the USGS land-surface database. The field is therefore marine exposure everywhere and will be too strong over land. Watch the distance-to-land figure when judging near-shore cases.
    • A known profile defect is reproduced deliberately. The continuity factor A in eq. (3) is the reciprocal of the continuous one in the reference Python, so the profile steps at ri whenever x1 ≠ x2. The JavaScript here copies the Python verbatim so the web preview and the GFE grid show the same field.

    Data

    • IBTrACS USA_* columns. North Atlantic and East Pacific are NHC values (hurdat_atl, hurdat_epa), archive window 2004–2024, chosen because 64 kt radii are reported as zero everywhere before 2004. West Pacific is a JTWC sample (jtwc_wp) over 2005–2024; the one-year difference is real and is not smoothed over here.
    • The archive is a bounded sample, not the whole record: at most 120 NHC storms drawn with a fixed seed and stratified across seasons and intensity, plus 100 West Pacific storms. It is enough to characterise behaviour, not enough to be a climatology.
    • RMW and ROCI are in nautical miles; distance to land is IBTrACS' own field in kilometres and is shown converted. Times are UTC and carry an explicit Z.
