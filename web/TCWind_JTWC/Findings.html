Findings: how well does the tool actually work?

A plain summary of how well this tool's storm-size and wind-field guesses match real JTWC data. This is a fixed summary, not a live view - to explore a real storm yourself, see Archived Cases in the live tool.

1Where this data comes from

  • Live tool ("Fetch bulletins" / "Paste text"): the same real-time JTWC warning bulletins this tool's GFE version uses in actual operations.
  • Archived Cases and everything on this page: JTWC's own after-the-fact review of each storm, done once the season is over. These are the corrected, final numbers - position, strength, wind radii, RMW (how far the strongest winds reach), and ROCI (how far the storm's whole circulation reaches) - from NOAA's public IBTrACS archive of past storms.
  • Years covered: 2001-2024. Not a range we chose - JTWC barely reported wind radii or RMW for Western Pacific storms before 2001, and 2025-2026 storms haven't been finalized yet.
  • In short: the live tool shows what it guesses in the moment, from a real-time warning. Everything below checks those same guesses against the true, later-corrected answer.

2What we found

Storm size · fixed

The size formula now guesses right on average

  • The original formula was built and tuned using Atlantic storms. Applied to Western Pacific tropical storms and typhoons, it guessed too small by about 6 nautical miles (nm) on average.
  • We rebuilt it using thousands of real Western Pacific storm records, then tested it against storms it hadn't seen before. The average miss dropped to about 2 nm, and it tracks the real answer more closely too.
  • This improved version is what the tool uses today.
Storm size · scope

The tool skips depressions entirely

  • Below tropical storm strength (under 34 kt), there's no organized wind field to model, and almost never any reported wind radii to build one from even if there were.
  • Rather than guess anyway, the tool now leaves the background wind grid completely untouched for a storm at depression strength - no invented structure where there isn't any real one.
  • Everything else on this page is scored only against tropical-storm-strength and stronger records, to match.
Wind field size · ROCI check

Runs small, worse for tropical storms than typhoons

  • We checked the size of the tool's wind field against ROCI - how far out a storm's whole circulation reaches, a separate measurement the tool never actually sees or uses.
  • For tropical storms, the tool's field runs 16 to 69 nm too small on average depending on which wind speed you check, and only loosely tracks the real size.
  • For typhoons, the field is closer at moderate wind speeds but stretches far too wide at the very lowest speeds checked - by nearly 80 nm too big on average.
Wind field shape

Least certain right next to the eye

  • We compared the tool's wind profile against a separate, independent formula (Holland, 1980), starting from the same storm strength and size.
  • The two disagree the most just outside the eyewall - by about 12 kt on average, and by as much as 68 kt in the worst cases - and agree more the farther out you go.
  • That's also the area - the eyewall - where getting the wind speed wrong matters most.
