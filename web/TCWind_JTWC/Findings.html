In one minute

This tool reads a JTWC (Joint Typhoon Warning Center) warning — position, intensity, and the 34/50/64 kt wind radii by quadrant — and builds a complete wind map from it using GTCM, NHC’s (National Hurricane Center’s) Gridded Tropical Cyclone Model: one smooth vortex shape fitted to those radii, in place of the straight-line-between-radii construction it replaced. Five plain statements below, each backed by one number pulled live from the measurement file behind this page. Full method and every other number are in the cards below and Technical detail.

The verdict

This tool renders a physically plausible wind field that is consistent with what a JTWC (Joint Typhoon Warning Center) bulletin says. It is not a forecast. It does not try to reproduce the bulletin’s wind radii exactly, on purpose.

It reads position, intensity and the 34 / 50 / 64 kt quadrant wind radii out of the bulletin. It fits the model to them: one symmetric modified Rankine vortex plus a wavenumber-1 asymmetry, by weighted least squares on wind error. One vortex cannot pass through twelve independently reported radii, so it does not try. The result is a single-peaked, corner-free field. Its gradient belongs to the model, not to wherever the four quadrant bisectors happen to fall.

The construction it replaced (per-quadrant) fitted each quadrant separately and hit every reported radius exactly. That left a slope break at every quadrant bisector, because interpolating reported radii around the compass puts a corner there by construction. The office chose the coherent field over the exact one. That is a trade, not a strict improvement, and both halves of it are on this page.

Across the archive, the modelled isotachs sit — from the radii the bulletins reported (mean signed error, — mean absolute error, — ring-quadrants). Roughly — of reported ring-quadrants are never reached by the modelled field at any radius. Those contribute no error at all to that average: the miss on those is total, and section 4 counts them separately.

If your decision turns on the exact radius the bulletin gave, read the bulletin. If it turns on the shape of the wind between and beyond those radii, this construction gives a smoother field than the one it replaced (see section 5). It does not give a more accurate one. The held-out prediction card above is the one test in this suite that scores a genuine prediction, on radii the fit was never shown. On that test the retired per-quadrant method matches or beats GTCM. GTCM sometimes misses a 64 kt ring outright where the per-quadrant method never does. Read that card before deciding what this tool is good for.

USE Reasonable uses

  • Getting a coherent first-guess wind field into GFE from a bulletin that carries only radii.
  • Judging the gradient and the shape of the envelope between the reported radii, where the bulletin says nothing.
  • Seeing the asymmetry implied by storm motion (Users Guide eq. (2), Schwerdt) rendered consistently.
  • Comparing one forecast hour with the next: the construction is the same at every hour, so changes are the bulletin’s, not the method’s.

DO NOT Not what it is for

  • Reading a radius off the field and quoting it as the R34/R50/R64 in the bulletin. It will be smaller: see section 2.
  • Anything over or near land. The boundary-layer / land roughness reduction is not implemented, so the field is marine exposure everywhere.
  • Wind outside the modelled 34 kt radius, where a local taper takes over that is not in the Users Guide.
  • Any claim of accuracy against observed wind. Nothing here measures that.

UNVERIFIED Known blind spots

  • No comparison against observations of any kind: not buoy, aircraft, scatterometer, or analysis.
  • Forecast hours (τ>0) are unverified: every number here comes from best-track analysis times.
  • The outer taper is a local heuristic, not a documented part of the model.

2Read this first: quadrant maximum vs quadrant average

This is the single most misreadable number on the page. Without it, the ring-fit table in section 3 looks like a systematic undersize of roughly a sixth, and the honest conclusion from that appearance would be that the tool is broken. It is not: the two numbers are measuring different things.

What the bulletin reports is the maximum extent of that wind speed anywhere in the quadrant: the farthest point at which, say, 34 kt is analysed in the northeast 90°.

What the GTCM fit aims at is the quadrant average radius, because the model is one smooth vortex and the guide specifies the fit that way (Users Guide step 2c). The reported radius is multiplied by — to get the fit target before the least squares is run. That factor is read from the shipped code when this page loads, not typed into it, so it cannot drift from the model it describes.

So a modelled isotach that lands about a sixth inside the reported radius is the model doing exactly what it was told. Section 3 therefore reports the same errors twice: against the reported radius, and against the target the fit was actually given. Neither number is the “real” one on its own; read them together.

4Rings the field never reaches

The error statistics in section 3 can only be computed where the modelled field actually crosses the threshold. Where one symmetric vortex cannot hold the reported structure, the field never reaches a reported ring at any radius along that bisector, and that ring-quadrant drops out of the average entirely. This section is where those cases are counted, so that the miss is visible rather than hidden in a smaller sample. The per-quadrant construction is zero here by construction.

Read this before quoting anything above.

Documented departures from the Users Guide

    Limits of the evidence on this page

    • No observations. Every number here compares the modelled field with a best-track analysis of storm structure. Best track is itself an analysis product, produced after the fact from the same kinds of data. It carries its own uncertainty in the wind radii, often tens of nautical miles. Agreement with it is internal consistency, not validation.
    • Analysis times only. Every case is a best-track analysis time. The tool is used on forecast bulletins at τ = 12…120 h, where position, intensity and radii are all forecasts. Nothing on this page verifies a forecast hour.
    • The interpolation between bulletin times is unmeasured. The live tool interpolates the track and the radii between forecast hours; no number here tests that path.
    • No land. The field is marine exposure everywhere, so it is too strong over land, and increasingly so with distance inland.
    • Sampling. The coherence and field-difference sections use a subsample of the archive, not every storm-time; the counts are in section 9.
