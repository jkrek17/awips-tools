The forecast recipe

How to turn all the model output into the most likely wind, the warning areas and the lows. Each step was tested on weeks held back from tuning, winter 2025-26, against ASCAT unless it says otherwise. The number beside each step is what it earned.

At a glance

  1. Blend the wind: GFS, ECMWF and the AI model at half weight, over the latest three runs.
  2. Lean into the spread: add a quarter of the spread, capped at 5 kt.
  3. Lift the cores, not the field: +5 kt where a storm core is likely, 34 kt where a gale core is.
  4. Draw warning areas from the strongest signal, never from the most likely wind.
  5. Let the members vote on the lows; trust their depth range, not their position spread.
  6. Read raw chances as low, and never draw from the 64 kt chance alone.
  7. Spend desk time on storm cores, day 1-2, above all in the Pacific.

1 Blend the wind

Average GFS, ECMWF and the AI model, with the AI model at half weight. Use the current run and the runs 12 h and 24 h older of each.

  • The blend is 0.6 to 1.9 kt closer to ASCAT than GFS or ECMWF alone in 25 kt+ water, days 1-5 (Atlantic winter).
  • Against the plain 3-model mean it raises gale skill (CSI) by .03 Atlantic, .04 Pacific, and cuts buoy error by 0.4 kt. That held on 40 Atlantic cycles it was never tuned on.
  • The AI model at half weight adds 2 to 3 points of storm catch in the Atlantic and 8 in the Pacific over full weight. AIFS runs about 4 kt low at gales in cold air.
  • The older runs add about 2 points of storm catch in both basins, but only with step 2. Lagging without the spread lean scores worse than the latest run alone.

2 Lean into the spread

Averaging flattens the peaks, so add a quarter of the spread across the runs or members (0.15 on day 1), capped at 5 kt. This is a deliberate lean toward roughly the 60th percentile, not a bias correction: it puts back the gale area that averaging removes.

  • Gale area goes from 0.7 to about 1.0 times what verifies.
  • Dropping it costs 7 to 8 points of storm catch.
  • Do not raise it for storm force: a bigger lean over-forecasts storm area. That is step 3's job.

3 Lift the cores, not the field

The models put the cores in the right place but run 2 to 6 kt under ASCAT's peaks, more with lead time. They are too smooth, not too shallow. So lift only where a core is likely:

  • +5 kt where the storm-core likelihood is 35% or more. Storm-force catch goes from 15% to 49% of ASCAT storm tiles, with 15% false alarms. On the GFS + ECMWF blend the same lift takes storm catch from 36% to 59% Atlantic, 22% to 36% Pacific.
  • Then 34 kt where the gale-core chance is 60% or more. Gale catch goes from 63% to 76% Atlantic at 1.2 times the ASCAT area, and 55% to 67% Pacific at 1.25 times.
  • +8 kt instead of +5 doubles the false alarms. Boosting the whole grid never paid.

The result is the page's Core Lifted wind: the most likely wind.

4 Warning areas from the strongest signal

The most likely wind is the wrong thing to warn from: it is built to be right on average, and a miss costs more than a false alarm. Each warning line comes from its own rule.

Gale

Strongest model at 30 kt. Covers about 97% of ASCAT gale water, at no more area than the desk's grids.

Storm

Core Lifted at 39 kt. Covers 93% of ASCAT storm water at 2.1 times its area. Desk grids draw 3.6 times, the issued text 5 to 6 times.

Hurricane force

Strongest model at 44 kt, where at least one member in a hundred has 64 kt nearby. Catches 81% of hurricane-force lows within 150 km (issued text 53% at the same area). About 4 in 5 of these areas hold no hurricane-force low: mostly storm cores, about 1 in 5 Greenland tip jets. That over-warning is on purpose.

  • Do not throw out a lone storm-force model. Alone at 48 kt it still verifies storm force within 50 km about 3 to 4 times in 5. GFS is the lone model 4 times in 5.

5 Let the members vote on the lows

Every ensemble member and deterministic run votes for its own lows. Keep a low where enough of them carry one, at their average position and median depth. Never take depth from the plain ensemble mean: it fills the lows by about 10 hPa by day 7.

  • Days 5-7, the vote beats ECMWF, GFS and AIFS at every lead: 26 to 100 km closer on position and 0.7 to 3.5 hPa closer on central pressure, with ECMWF's rate of false lows.
  • Trust the depth range. The members' range holds the analysed pressure about 4 times in 5 and widens when the error does.
  • Ignore the position spread. It does not predict the position error. Draw one ring per lead that holds half the lows (about 320, 370, 410 km at day 5, 6, 7).
  • Days 1-5: the ensemble low track with a cone of 1.2 times the members' spread holds the low about 2 times in 3 (roughly 75 km wide at day 1, 375 km at day 5).

6 Read the chances as low

  • A raw 30% gale share is about 40% true. In the Atlantic a raw 7% storm share is about 30% true. The page shows the corrected chances.
  • A storm chance of 50% or more verifies about 95% of the time within 100 km.
  • The 64 kt chance has no skill cell by cell and cannot be corrected. Never draw from it alone.
  • For hurricane force, the blend's own peak sorts the cores: about 5% reach 64 kt under a 50 kt peak, 43% at 50-55 kt, 75% above 55 kt.

7 Where desk time pays

  • Storm cores. On day 1 the desk catches 64% of ASCAT storm-force cells, GFS 26%, the plain blend 11%. This is the one place the forecaster clearly beats the models, and Core Lifted is built to close that gap.
  • The Pacific. Pacific storm force is poorly forecast by every method; the desk's grids are the tightest there.
  • Not ordinary water. 85% of the water is routine (all models under 30 kt) and takes 83% of the edits, yet there the blend is 1 to 2 kt closer to ASCAT than the finished grid.

How to show it: one map

Layered in the order a forecaster reads it. Wind is the only fill; everything else is a line or a mark, so nothing hides the wind.

  1. Most likely wind (Core Lifted) as the colour fill, in the page's wind colours.
  2. Warning areas as dashed outlines, one colour per hazard: gale yellow, storm orange, hurricane force pink.
  3. "Possible" lines as thinner solid lines in the same colours.
  4. Lows as the ensemble track and cone, with the GFS, ECMWF and AI model centres as small marks, since a split between the model families is the thing to notice.
  5. Pressure as light isobars from the 3-model mean, every 4 hPa, and a label on each low with its depth range, such as L 968 (960-978).

A 24 h run-change layer shows the trend. Read it as a glance, not a lean: the truth comes back about a fifth of the way.

What to watch for

  • Winter-trained. One winter, November to March, 30-67N. Not tested in the subtropics or on tropical cyclones.
  • March to October the core likelihood is weaker and middle chances run 7 to 10 points high, so Core Lifted over-warns more.
  • Fast bombs with a timing split. When the model families are 12-18 h apart, the blend averages two lows and Core Lifted tops out near 45 kt. Read the warning lines for that storm.
  • Hurricane-force cores. Even Core Lifted rarely reaches 64 kt; near hurricane-force lows the models run 6 to 16 kt under ASCAT. The warning line, not the wind, carries that risk.
  • Pacific scores are thin. ASCAT sees about 4% of the Pacific at 00/12Z, so Pacific results lean on the 06/18Z passes, and Pacific hurricane force cannot be tested.
  • Along ice and steep coasts ASCAT is blind in the first 25-30 km, where barrier jets peak.

Tried and dropped

Each of these was tested and lost to the recipe. Check here before trying one again.

  • 30 m winds or a stability switch
  • Fronts, jets, quadrant or storm-type clues
  • Extra lift where lows deepen fast
  • Boosting the whole grid
  • Weighting models by recent skill
  • Retraining the core model; +8 kt lift
  • Position ellipses for lows
  • Member-backed storm line
  • Wind built from the pressure gradient
  • Shifting the field to follow the low
  • Machine-learned blend
