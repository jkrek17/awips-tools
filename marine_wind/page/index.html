How the blended wind is made

Blended wind = a weighted average of the GFS, ECMWF and AIFS 10 m wind speeds, plus a quarter of the spread of about 80 ensemble members (31 GEFS and 50 or 51 ECMWF ENS), capped at 5 kt. At +24 h and earlier it adds 15% of the spread instead. Direction comes from the average wind vector.

Each model is weighted at each point by how many ensemble members are within about 5 kt of it, so a model out on its own counts for less. On 200 test cases this trimmed the error in 25+ kt winds by about 0.1 kt over a plain average.

Averaging three models flattens the peaks, so the plain average forecasts only about 70% of the gale area that verifies. Adding a little of the spread restores it. Tested on 345 past North Atlantic cases from three seasons, scored against the GFS and ECMWF analyses and against buoys and ships:

  • Gale area forecast rises from about 72% to 97% of what verified.
  • Gale hit score (CSI at 34 kt) improves by 0.026, and storm force (48 kt) by about 0.04.
  • Error where winds are 25 kt or more drops by about 0.4 kt against buoys.
  • It costs a little accuracy in light winds, and storm cores can still be several knots off. It cannot create a storm the models don't have.

A second test on 733 cases per basin (Sep 2025 to Oct 2026) found the same recipe works in the North Pacific without retuning: gale hit score up about 0.04 there and about 0.03 in the Atlantic, and about 0.4 kt less error at buoys in both. Scored as a headline for each zone-sized area, gale headlines improved by 0.04 to 0.07 and storm headlines by 0.06 to 0.10.

Focus

Ride: every model under 30 kt. Agree: the models agree on the wind category. Focus: the models split across 34 kt, or differ by 10 kt or more near gale. High impact: they split across 48 or 64 kt. A model counts as agreeing if it has that wind within about 50 km.

Lone-model signals

Outlined areas where only one model has storm (48 kt) or hurricane (64 kt) force and neither other model has it within about 50 km. In testing on both basins, a lone storm-force area verified 11–16% of the time and a lone hurricane-force area 0–2%. GFS is the lone model about 80% of the time. When fewer than 5% of members reach 48 kt the lone signal almost never verified (1–2%); when half or more do, it verified 79–88% of the time.

Chance of 34, 48, 64 kt

Built from the ensemble members at that grid point: the 31 GEFS and about 50 ECMWF ENS members plus the 51-member AIFS ensemble. Adding the AIFS ensemble made these chances 6% (34 kt) and 11% (48 kt) more accurate in testing. It is left out of the blended wind, where its smaller spread and smoother mean did not help.

The gale chance is the plain share of members. The storm and hurricane-force chances are that share adjusted to how often it verified in 2025–26, by basin and lead time. Raw member shares understate likely storms and overstate unlikely ones: in testing, 40–50% of members at 48 kt verified 54–79% of the time at that point and about 95% within 100 km, while 10–20% verified about 7%. The adjustment cut the day 1–2 storm-force error by about 15% on months it was not fitted to. Hover a point to see the raw member shares too. The 64 kt chance is still poor at a single point; read it as somewhere in this storm, not here.

Change since yesterday's run

The 3-model average wind now minus the same models' run 24 h earlier, valid at the same time. It is shown only where either run has 25 kt or more, so light-wind noise doesn't hide the changes that matter. In testing, the truth fell back about a fifth of this change toward the earlier run. Where two runs in a row weakened a system, it verified 4–5 kt stronger than the latest run. A run that just jumped is worth a second look, and a system that two runs in a row weakened should not be dropped too quickly.

Data: NOAA GFS and GEFS and ECMWF open data, from the AWS open-data mirrors, 0.25 degree grid, 30–67N, 75W–0 (Atlantic) and 160E–115W (Pacific). Updated twice a day. Guidance only, not an official forecast.
